Couples therapy offers a neutral, structured space for two people to understand each other better, work through conflict, and rebuild connection. Whether you're facing a specific crisis or want to strengthen your relationship, couples counseling can help you navigate challenges together.

## When couples therapy helps

Couples counseling is beneficial for:

- **Communication breakdowns** — when conversations escalate quickly or shut down entirely
- **Conflict patterns** — repeating arguments about the same issues without resolution
- **Life transitions** — moving in together, marriage, children, job changes, or retirement
- **Infidelity or betrayal** — rebuilding trust after a breach in the relationship
- **Intimacy concerns** — physical or emotional disconnection
- **Different values or life goals** — exploring whether you're still aligned
- **Neurodivergent relationships** — partners with ADHD or autism navigating different communication or sensory needs
- **LGBTQIA+ and non-traditional structures** — affirming support for couples in diverse relationship forms
- **Divorce or separation support** — untangling shared lives or co-parenting after a split

Couples therapy is **not** a last resort. Many couples benefit most when they seek support early, before resentment hardens or disconnection feels permanent.

## What to expect in couples counseling

A couples therapist's role is to remain neutral and help both partners feel heard. The process typically includes:

1. **Initial sessions** — exploring the relationship history, current concerns, and each person's perspective
2. **Skills building** — learning new communication patterns, conflict resolution, and empathy techniques
3. **Deep work** — identifying underlying patterns, unmet needs, and attachment dynamics
4. **Moving forward** — developing shared goals and practices to maintain progress

Sessions are usually 60 minutes, and the pacing depends on your needs — some couples meet weekly, others every other week. The goal is to give you tools and insights you can use between sessions and long after therapy ends.

## Couples therapy and insurance

**Most health insurance does not cover couples counseling.** Here's why:

Insurance typically reimburses mental health services tied to a diagnosed condition — such as depression, anxiety, or PTSD. Relationship concerns are often coded with **Z codes** (relational problems) rather than F-code diagnoses, and insurance plans treat these differently. Even when one or both partners have active mental health coverage, couples work is usually not reimbursed under those plans.

This means couples counseling is handled on a **self-pay basis** in most cases, even when both partners have insurance. It's worth asking your provider directly, but plan to budget for self-pay rates when seeking couples therapy.
