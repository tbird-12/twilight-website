> **Disclaimer:** CPT and HCPCS coding depends on medical necessity, documentation, payer rules, and the services actually provided. This page is for general education only and is not a guarantee of coverage or payment.

If you have received services with our psychiatric Nurse Practitioner, your [Explanation of Benefits (EOB)](/blog/billing/understanding-your-eob) may show codes that look different from therapy or testing codes. Medication management visits use **Evaluation and Management (E/M)** codes — a different code family from psychotherapy — along with add-ons when psychotherapy is provided during the same encounter. This guide explains each of the codes you are most likely to see.

Insurance and Kentucky Medicaid are accepted for medication management services. See our [accepted insurance page](/resources/insurances-accepted) for the current panel list, and our [Medication Management](/services/medication-management) service page for a general overview.

For therapy billing codes, see our [therapy CPT codes guide](/blog/billing/cpt-codes-therapy-services). For psychological testing codes, see our [psychological testing CPT codes guide](/blog/billing/common-cpt-codes-used-in-psychological-testing).

## Medication management codes at a glance

| Code | What it usually means | Typical billing pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **99205** | New patient office visit, high complexity | First appointment, complex presentation |
| **99204** | New patient office visit, moderate complexity | First appointment, moderate-complexity presentation |
| **99214** | Established patient office visit, moderate complexity | Routine follow-up with prescription review and adjustment |
| **99213** | Established patient office visit, low complexity | Brief established-patient follow-up |
| **99215** | Established patient office visit, high complexity | High-complexity established-patient visit |
| **90833** | Psychotherapy add-on, 30 minutes | Billed alongside an E/M code when psychotherapy is provided in the same encounter |
| **90785** | Interactive complexity add-on | Added to 90833 (or a psychotherapy code) when specific complicating factors are present |
| **H0032** | Mental health service plan development | HCPCS code often required by Medicaid for treatment planning services |

## Understanding E/M codes

### What makes a visit "new" vs. "established"?

The first group of codes (99205, 99204) applies to **new patients** — individuals who have not been seen by any clinician in our practice within the past three years. All subsequent visits use **established patient** codes (99213, 99214, 99215).

### How complexity level is determined

The complexity level (low, moderate, high) is determined by the **Medical Decision Making (MDM)** documented in the visit note or by total time, including preparation and post-encounter work. Complexity depends on:

- **Number and nature of problems** — stable chronic conditions vs. new or worsening conditions
- **Amount and complexity of data reviewed** — records, test results, medication history
- **Risk of complications** — including the risk profile of the medications being managed

Because these are clinical documentation decisions, the same type of appointment can warrant different codes depending on what was medically addressed that day.

## Code-by-code explanations

### 99205 — New patient, high complexity

**99205** is used for a first appointment that involves **high-complexity medical decision making** or a total encounter time typically of 60–74 minutes. A high-complexity new-patient psychiatric visit often involves:

- Multiple new psychiatric diagnoses
- Starting controlled substances (e.g., stimulants for ADHD, Schedule IV/V medications)
- Significant psychiatric history requiring extensive record review
- Coordination with other prescribers or specialists

You are most likely to see **99205** on the claim for your first medication management appointment if your presentation was clinically complex.

### 99204 — New patient, moderate complexity

**99204** is used for a first appointment with **moderate-complexity medical decision making** or a total encounter time typically of 45–59 minutes. A moderate-complexity new-patient visit might involve:

- One new well-characterized psychiatric condition
- A medication recommendation with moderate risk
- Consideration of test results or external records

If your initial visit was your first appointment but less complex than a 99205 level encounter, **99204** is the appropriate code.

### 99214 — Established patient, moderate complexity

**99214** is the most common code for **routine follow-up visits** in psychiatric medication management. It covers moderate-complexity established-patient encounters, typically 30–39 minutes total time, and reflects:

- Reviewing and adjusting an existing medication regimen
- Assessing response to treatment and side effects
- Managing a stable chronic condition with ongoing medication needs
- Ordering or reviewing labs (e.g., serum levels, metabolic panels)

Most regularly scheduled medication management follow-ups will appear as **99214** on your EOB.

### 99213 — Established patient, low complexity

**99213** reflects a brief, low-complexity follow-up visit, typically 20–29 minutes total time. This code may be used when:

- The patient is stable and doing well on their current regimen
- The visit requires minimal data review or medication adjustment
- A shorter check-in is clinically appropriate

### 99215 — Established patient, high complexity

**99215** is reserved for follow-up visits that are significantly more complex, typically 40–54 minutes total time. Examples include:

- Unexpected deterioration requiring a change in the treatment plan
- Introduction of a new controlled substance to an established regimen
- Managing a psychiatric crisis or significant adverse medication effect
- Coordination with inpatient providers or other specialists following a hospitalization

If you see **99215** on a follow-up claim, it reflects that the encounter that day was more involved than a routine check-in.

## Add-on codes used with E/M visits

### 90833 — Psychotherapy, 30 minutes (E/M add-on)

One of the most important things to understand about psychiatric billing is that **an E/M code and a psychotherapy code can be billed on the same date of service** when psychotherapy is provided *in addition to* the medical management portion of the visit.

**90833** is the add-on code for **30 minutes of psychotherapy** provided alongside an E/M service. It is never billed alone — it always appears alongside one of the E/M codes listed above.

A typical combined claim looks like this:

```
99214   Established patient E/M, moderate complexity
90833   + Psychotherapy, 30 minutes (add-on)
```

This reflects a visit where the Nurse Practitioner both managed medications *and* provided structured psychotherapy as part of the same encounter. The two codes describe genuinely different work: one is medical management, the other is psychotherapeutic intervention.

### 90785 — Interactive complexity (add-on)

**90785** is an add-on code that can be billed alongside **90833** when specific, documented complicating factors were present during the psychotherapy portion of the encounter. This code is never billed alone.

Qualifying situations include:

- Use of play, drawing, or physical objects as a primary communication tool (common with children)
- Involvement of legally mandated third parties (e.g., court-ordered treatment)
- Significant communication barriers or maladaptive communication patterns
- Evidence-based adaptive communication techniques required for the session

If **90785** appears on a claim alongside an E/M code and **90833**, it indicates the combined encounter included documented interactive complexity during the therapy portion.

### H0032 — Mental health service plan development

**H0032** is a **HCPCS Level II code** required by some Kentucky Medicaid managed care organizations for the clinician's work developing or updating a **formal treatment plan**. It is not a CPT code and is specific to certain payers — primarily Medicaid MCOs.

If you are a Medicaid client and see **H0032** on your paperwork, it reflects clinician documentation required by your plan — not a separate appointment. This is a standard component of Medicaid-compliant psychiatric care.

## How a combined E/M + psychotherapy visit looks on a claim

A patient receiving both medication management and psychotherapy in the same appointment might have a claim like:

```
99214   Established patient office visit, moderate complexity
90833   Psychotherapy, 30 minutes (add-on to E/M)
```

Or, if a child's session included play-based communication:

```
99214   Established patient office visit, moderate complexity
90833   Psychotherapy, 30 minutes (add-on)
90785   Interactive complexity (add-on to 90833)
```

These combinations are not billing errors. The codes reflect the full scope of the clinical encounter.

## Telehealth and medication management

Medication management visits at Twilight Psychology may be conducted via telehealth. The same E/M codes (99205, 99204, 99214, 99213, 99215) apply to synchronous telehealth encounters. Most major insurance carriers and Kentucky Medicaid cover telehealth medication management at the same rate as in-person visits, subject to your plan's specific rules. ## Insurance and Medicaid coverage

> **Insurance and Kentucky Medicaid accepted** for medication management services. Coverage details depend on your specific plan.

Most commercial insurance plans and Kentucky Medicaid managed care organizations cover outpatient E/M services. Coverage for the **90833** psychotherapy add-on is generally the same as standalone psychotherapy coverage under your plan.

Controlled substance prescriptions (e.g., stimulants for ADHD) are subject to your pharmacy benefit, which is separate from your medical benefit — your EOB from a pharmacy visit will look different from one for an office visit.

If you have questions about coverage for a specific code, our billing team can help. [Contact us](/contact) before or after your visit.
