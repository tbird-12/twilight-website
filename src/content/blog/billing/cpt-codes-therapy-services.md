When you receive a superbill or an [Explanation of Benefits (EOB)](/blog/billing/understanding-your-eob) from your insurance carrier, a column of five-digit codes can be hard to interpret. This guide focuses on the codes most commonly used for **outpatient therapy** — individual sessions, family work, and the add-on codes that sometimes appear alongside them. Insurance and Kentucky Medicaid are accepted for therapy services; see our [accepted insurance page](/resources/insurances-accepted) for the current panel list.

If you are looking for codes used in **psychological testing**, see our [psychological testing CPT codes guide](/blog/billing/common-cpt-codes-used-in-psychological-testing). For medication management codes, see our [medication management CPT codes guide](/blog/billing/cpt-codes-medication-management).

## Therapy CPT codes at a glance

| Code | What it usually means | Typical billing pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **90791** | Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation / intake | Billed once at the start of therapy to establish diagnosis and treatment plan |
| **90837** | Individual psychotherapy, 60 minutes | Standard full-session therapy code |
| **90834** | Individual psychotherapy, 45 minutes | Used when the face-to-face session is approximately 38–52 minutes |
| **90832** | Individual psychotherapy, 30 minutes | Used when the face-to-face session is approximately 16–37 minutes |
| **90846** | Family psychotherapy without the patient present | Used for parent/caregiver sessions when the identified patient is not present |
| **90785** | Interactive complexity add-on | Added to a psychotherapy code when specific complicating factors are present |
| **96127** | Brief emotional/behavioral assessment | Standardized screening tool administered during an encounter |
| **H0032** | Mental health service plan development | HCPCS code often required by Medicaid for treatment planning services |

## Code-by-code explanations

### 90791 — Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation

Code **90791** is the initial diagnostic interview that begins the therapy relationship. The clinician gathers a comprehensive history, identifies presenting concerns, establishes a working diagnosis, and documents a plan for ongoing care.

This code is typically billed **once** at the very start of therapy. If you are transitioning to a new clinician or returning after a long break, a new 90791 may be appropriate. Most insurance plans, including Kentucky Medicaid plans, cover this service.

### 90837 — Individual psychotherapy, 60 minutes

**90837** is the most common code used for a standard 60-minute individual therapy session. It requires face-to-face (or synchronous telehealth) contact and covers a session of approximately 53 minutes or more of psychotherapy.

Because it represents a full hour of clinical work, **90837 is the standard code for most ongoing therapy visits** at Twilight Psychology.

### 90834 — Individual psychotherapy, 45 minutes

**90834** covers a therapy session of approximately 38–52 minutes. It is used when the clinical encounter is shorter than a full hour but longer than a brief check-in.

You may see **90834** on your bill when a session ran shorter than planned, when the appointment was scheduled as a 45-minute visit, or when a feedback session following an evaluation falls within this time range.

### 90832 — Individual psychotherapy, 30 minutes

**90832** covers brief individual therapy sessions of approximately 16–37 minutes. This code is less common for routine outpatient therapy, but it may appear when:

- A session is abbreviated due to a client arriving late or needing to leave early
- A clinician provides a brief clinical contact separate from a longer visit on the same date

### 90846 — Family psychotherapy without the patient present

Code **90846** is used when the clinician meets with a parent, caregiver, or other family member **without the identified patient present** and the session has a therapeutic, treatment-focused purpose.

Common examples include:
- Parent sessions for a child in therapy to discuss behavioral strategies and generalization of skills
- Caregiver education and support
- Collateral contact with a family member that constitutes a full clinical session

If the identified patient **is** present for a family-format session, a different code (90847) would typically be used. **90846** specifically refers to the "without patient" format.

### 90785 — Interactive complexity (add-on)

**90785** is an **add-on code** that can be billed alongside 90837, 90834, 90832, or 90846 when the session includes specific, documented complicating factors. It is never billed alone.

Qualifying conditions include:

- The use of play equipment, physical devices, or drawing as part of the session (often with children)
- Involvement of third parties (e.g., court-ordered treatment) that significantly complicate the therapy
- Communication barriers or significant maladaptive communication (not just general difficulty)
- Evidence-based use of adaptive measures to communicate with the patient

If 90785 appears on your bill alongside a therapy code, it reflects that the session included one or more of these documented complexities — it is not an error.

### 96127 — Brief emotional/behavioral assessment

**96127** covers the administration, scoring, and interpretation of a **brief standardized screening tool** during a therapy or clinical encounter. Common examples include:

- Depression screening instruments (e.g., PHQ-9, PHQ-A)
- Anxiety screening instruments (e.g., GAD-7, SCARED)
- ADHD symptom rating scales
- Behavioral symptom checklists

Each completed instrument may be billed as one unit of **96127**. This code is distinct from the more comprehensive psychological testing codes (96136, 96130) and simply reflects the use of a validated brief measure to guide clinical decision-making during the visit.

### H0032 — Mental health service plan development

**H0032** is a **HCPCS Level II code** (rather than a CPT code) that is required by some Medicaid managed care organizations in Kentucky for **treatment plan development and review**. HCPCS codes like this one are specific to certain payers and are not typically seen on commercial insurance claims.

If you are a Medicaid client and see **H0032** on your paperwork, it reflects the clinician's documented work creating or updating a treatment plan as required by your plan's billing requirements. This is part of coordinated Medicaid-compliant care, not a separate clinical appointment.

## Why multiple codes can appear for a single visit

A single therapy session can generate more than one code. The most common pattern is a **primary psychotherapy code plus an add-on**:

```
90837   Individual therapy, 60 minutes
90785   + Interactive complexity add-on
```

Another common pattern involves a **brief assessment administered during the session**:

```
90837   Individual therapy, 60 minutes
96127   + Brief emotional/behavioral assessment (one screening tool)
```

Seeing two codes for one appointment is not duplicate billing. The codes describe different components of the professional work performed that day.

## How therapy codes are selected

The clinician selects the therapy code based on the **actual time** of the face-to-face session:

| Actual face-to-face time | Code used |
|---|---|
| 16–37 minutes | 90832 |
| 38–52 minutes | 90834 |
| 53 minutes or more | 90837 |

Payer rules vary, and some plans have additional documentation or prior authorization requirements. If you have questions about a specific line item, our billing team is happy to help — [contact us](/contact).
