> **Disclaimer:** Billing rules vary by payer, license type, and state. The information below is for general education. Verify all rules directly with your malpractice carrier, your state licensing board, and each payer before submitting claims.


Before a single claim can be submitted several foundational requirements must be in place. The claim will be signed by, and adjudicated under, a specific clinician. That clinician must be credentialed with the payer, must hold a valid NPI number, and must have the appropriate licensure and taxonomy code configured correctly. When those pieces do not align, the claim will be denied regardless of how accurately the CPT codes are selected.

This post is the first in a series on billing for mental health services in a Kentucky private practice setting. The credentialing requirements described here apply whether the services involve [evaluations](/services/psychological-evaluations/), [therapy](/services/therapy/), [medication management](/services/medication-management/), or [telehealth](/services/telehealth/). ## NPI Numbers: NPI-1 and NPI-2

Every clinician who bills insurance needs a **National Provider Identifier (NPI)**. NPIs are issued by CMS through the NPPES registry and are free to apply for. There are two types.

### NPI Type 1 (Individual Provider NPI)

An **NPI-1** is assigned to an individual clinician — a psychologist, therapist, prescriber, or other licensed provider. It is tied to the person, not the practice, and it follows the clinician from employer to employer. Each clinician has exactly one NPI-1.

Key points:
- Required for any clinician who bills independently
- The NPI-1 is what appears in Box 24J on a CMS-1500 claim form (the rendering provider NPI)
- Applying is done at [nppes.cms.hhs.gov](https://nppes.cms.hhs.gov)
- Processing typically takes 2–10 business days

### NPI Type 2 (Organizational NPI)

An **NPI-2** is assigned to a group, practice, or organization. In a group private practice, the NPI-2 is used in the billing provider field (Box 33a on the CMS-1500). The NPI-2 does not replace the individual NPI-1 — both may appear on the same claim.

Key points:
- The group NPI-2 must also be credentialed with each payer separately
- Each practice location may have its own NPI-2 if billing locations differ
- If your practice has multiple clinicians, the group NPI-2 is what creates a shared billing entity

At Twilight Psychology, licensed individual providers hold the relevant individual NPI-1 and the practice maintains a group NPI-2 for claim submission purposes.

## Credentialing with Payers

Having an NPI number is necessary but not sufficient. Before billing a commercial insurance plan, government program, or Medicaid managed care plan — for any service, including therapy, psychological testing, and medication management — the clinician must be **credentialed and contracted** with that specific payer.

### What Credentialing Involves

- Verifying the provider's license, education, and training
- Confirming malpractice coverage
- Reviewing any disciplinary history through NPDB (National Practitioner Data Bank) queries
- Executing a participation agreement that sets out the billing rules and fee schedule

Credentialing timelines vary widely. Some payers complete credentialing in 30 days; others take 90 to 180 days. During that waiting period, the clinician **cannot bill the payer** for services, even if the patient has already been seen.

### Retroactive Billing After Credentialing

Some payers will allow retroactive billing back to the effective date of credentialing once the process is finalized. The specific rules vary by plan. If a patient is seen before credentialing is complete, it is important to understand the payer's retroactive billing policy before assuming those claims can be recovered.

### Re-Credentialing

Most payers require periodic re-credentialing, typically every two or three years. Failing to re-credential on time can result in claims being denied or the clinician being removed from the network. Re-credentialing requirements apply to all license types and service categories — psychologists, therapists, and prescribers alike.

## Taxonomy Codes

A **taxonomy code** classifies the type of provider billing the claim. The taxonomy code must match the payer's records for the NPI, and it must accurately reflect the clinician's license type and specialty.

Commonly used taxonomy codes for behavioral health in Kentucky include:

| Code | Provider Type |
|---|---|
| **103T00000X** | Psychologist |
| **103TP0016X** | Psychologist — pediatric |
| **1223P0300X** | Neuropsychologist |
| **363LP0808X** | Psychiatric/mental health nurse practitioner |

The taxonomy code appears in **Box 24I** of the CMS-1500 form. If the code submitted does not match the taxonomy on file with the payer for that NPI, the claim will typically be rejected or denied.

When setting up a new provider in your billing system, verify the taxonomy code in the payer's enrollment system and in NPPES. Mismatches are one of the most common avoidable denial reasons for new clinicians joining a practice — whether they primarily bill therapy, evaluation, or medication management codes.

## Incident-to Billing

**Incident-to billing** is a Medicare concept that allows a non-physician provider's services to be billed under a supervising physician's NPI when specific conditions are met. In mental health contexts this is sometimes misunderstood, so a few clarifications are important.

### When Incident-to Applies (Medicare Only)

Under traditional Medicare Part B, incident-to billing allows services by clinical staff (nurses, therapists, counselors under certain circumstances) to be billed at **100% of the physician or qualified non-physician practitioner fee schedule** rather than at the reduced rate that applies when billing under the individual clinician's own NPI.

Requirements for incident-to under Medicare:
1. The supervising physician or qualified NPP must have seen the patient first and established the treatment plan
2. The supervising provider must be **present in the office suite** (not necessarily in the room) when the service is provided
3. The service must be within the scope of the employee's license and the supervisor's specialty
4. The service must be an integral part of the established plan of care

### Incident-to Does Not Apply to Psychological Testing

Psychological testing (96130, 96131, 96136, 96137) billed to Medicare requires the service to be performed by a licensed psychologist or other qualified health professional. Incident-to billing is **not available** for psychological testing under Medicare because the testing services are specifically limited to qualified health professionals who are independently eligible to bill those codes.

For commercial insurance and Medicaid, incident-to rules do not apply — payer contracts govern supervision requirements instead.

## Supervisory Billing in Private Practice

Outside of Medicare's incident-to framework, private practices may employ provisionally licensed clinicians (T-LPs, T-LPAs in Kentucky) who can provide services under the supervision of a fully licensed provider. This applies across **all clinical service types** — therapy sessions, psychological evaluations, and in some cases medication management visits.

In Kentucky, the following apply:

- A **Temporary Licensed Psychological Associate (T-LPA)** may perform psychological testing under the supervision of a Licensed Psychologist (LP).
- A **Licensed Psychological Associate (LPA)** may perform testing independently within their scope of practice.
- A **Licensed Professional Counselor Associate (LPCA)** may provide therapy services under the supervision of a Licensed Professional Clinical Counselor (LPCC-S).
- Supervision requirements for billing purposes vary by payer — some payers require the supervising psychologist or counselor to co-sign and bill the claim; others will credential and pay the associate directly.

Before billing under a supervising provider's NPI for services performed by a supervisee, verify the payer's specific supervision and billing rules. This varies significantly between Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial plans. Billing under the supervisor's NPI when the payer requires individual credentialing for the supervisee is a billing compliance risk for any service type.

## The Signed Claim and the Rendering Provider

On every claim submitted at Twilight Psychology, the **rendering provider** (the clinician who actually performed the service) is identified in Box 24J of the CMS-1500. The **billing provider** in Box 33 reflects the group practice.

All claims at Twilight Psychology are reviewed and signed by **Dr. Heather Cornett** before submission. This review step is part of the practice's billing compliance process and ensures that the codes, units, and provider information on the claim accurately reflect the services documented in the clinical record — for therapy, evaluations, and medication management alike.

## What This Means for Claim Approval

Getting the foundational billing details right — valid NPIs, correct taxonomy codes, current credentialing status — reduces denials before coding even begins. A claim with perfect CPT codes will still be denied if the NPI is unregistered with the payer, if the taxonomy code does not match, or if the rendering provider is not yet credentialed.
