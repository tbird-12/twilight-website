Accepting insurance is one of the most meaningful things a private practice can do for accessibility — and it's something Twilight Psychology has always taken seriously. But insurance isn't a simple yes or no. Different plans pay very differently across different service types, and over time, some of those gaps have grown too wide to bridge.

**Effective December 1, 2026**, we are making targeted changes to which insurances we accept for certain services. These changes are specific — they don't affect all of what we do — and we want to explain them clearly so you can plan accordingly.

## Aetna Better Health — no longer accepted for psychological testing

Starting December 1, 2026, **Twilight Psychology will no longer accept Aetna Better Health for new psychological testing appointments.** This is in addition to Wellcare of KY Medicaid, Anthem commercial, CHAMPVA plans, which we have announced we will no longer accept for new testing appointments. Any evaluation already scheduled before that date will be honored.

This decision was not easy. We're aware that many families who rely on Aetna Better Health are navigating foster care systems, DCBS involvement, and other complex circumstances — and that access to evaluation services matters deeply for those kids and families. We didn't come to this decision lightly.

The reality is that Aetna Better Health's policies for evaluations, frequent denials for psychological testing, the administrative burden of communicating these concerns with the case manager, clinicians, clients; and reimbursement rates for testing, combined with recurring issues around clawbacks and claim disputes on evaluations, has made it financially unsustainable for us to continue accepting new testing cases under this plan.

**What this does NOT affect:**
- Therapy with Aetna Better Health — no change
- Medication management with Aetna Better Health — no change

**If you or a client needs a testing evaluation and is covered by Aetna Better Health:**
- Self-pay is available for non-contracted providers; [our current rates are listed here](/resources/fees)
- We're happy to provide a referral to another practice if that's more appropriate
- Switching to a different MCO (Managed Care Organization) that we accept may be an option for some families
- DCBS or a referring agency may be able to authorize payment outside of the Medicaid plan

## Cigna — no longer accepted for new therapy clients

Starting December 1, 2026, **we will no longer accept Cigna for new therapy clients.** We will continue seeing all current Cigna therapy clients — this change applies only to new intakes.

The reason is straightforward: Cigna's reimbursement for therapy is the lowest of any commercial insurer we work with. Their current rates — under $80 for intake and for 53 minute individual sessions — fall below even Medicaid reimbursement rates for a pre-licensed clinician. This is the same rate for licensed clinicians. Every other commercial insurance we participate with pays over $100 per session. Negotiating higher rates with Cigna has not been successful, which leaves us with no choice but to stop accepting them for new therapy clients.

We want to be able to offer sustainable, high-quality therapy targeting the needs of our clients with neurodivergence. Rates this low make that difficult to sustain to attract and retain skilled clinicians, which ultimately impacts the quality of care we can provide.

**What this does NOT affect:**
- Psychological testing with Cigna — no change
- Medication management with Cigna — no change

If you're a current Cigna therapy client at Twilight Psychology, your care is not disrupted. If you are a new client looking to use Cigna for therapy, we will be glad to help you find another provider who can serve you, or discuss self-pay options with you directly.


## What these changes mean for Twilight Psychology, longer term

The direct outcome is improved sustainability. Firstly, testing is resource-intensive, the CPT codes that cover it haven't been updated in years, and Medicaid reimbursement doesn't keep pace with the actual cost of delivering a high-quality evaluation. We've absorbed that gap for a long time and are one of the few practices who offer comprehensive psychological testing to medicaid clients. Continuing to do so at scale isn't something we can sustain without compromising the quality and depth of our work. Secondly, it is extremely challenging to find skilled clinicians who are willing and able to provide thorough evaluations under the current reimbursement structure of insurance plans.

We remain committed to neurodiverse-affirming, thorough evaluations — and to being the practice that finds what others miss. These changes are about protecting our ability to keep doing that work.

## Questions about your coverage

If you're unsure how these changes affect you or someone you're referring, please don't hesitate to reach out. Our intake team can verify benefits, discuss options, and help you figure out the best path forward.
