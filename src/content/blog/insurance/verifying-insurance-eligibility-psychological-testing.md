> **Disclaimer:** Eligibility verification is a point-in-time confirmation. Coverage can change after verification. Always re-verify for each date of service and before each new service type. This post is for general education and not a guarantee of coverage or payment.

Confirming that a client's insurance is active is not the same as confirming that insurance will **pay for a specific mental health service**. Many practices learn this distinction the hard way — after a [therapy](/services/therapy/) or [evaluation](/services/psychological-evaluations/) has begun, or after a [medication management](/services/medication-management/) visit, when the claim is denied because the plan never covered the service or because pre-authorization was required and not obtained.

This is the fourth post in our series on billing for mental health services in Kentucky. It covers how to verify eligibility effectively, what information to collect, and how coordination of benefits affects the claim before it is ever submitted.

## Why Eligibility Verification Matters Before Mental Health Services

For straightforward therapy or medication management visits, an eligibility check at the start of care is often sufficient. Psychological testing — and other services with higher total costs or prior authorization requirements — warrants more thorough verification because:

- **Benefit limitations apply.** Many plans have annual limits on psychological testing hours, authorized therapy sessions, or require a specific diagnosis to authorize certain services.
- **Pre-authorization may be required.** For [96130 and 96131 evaluation codes](/blog/billing/common-cpt-codes-used-in-psychological-testing), Aetna Better Health, UHC, WellCare, and Ambetter require prior authorization before services can be billed. Some plans also require PA for initial psychiatric medication evaluations. Testing or services delivered without authorization = non-covered service = write-off or patient balance.
- **Out-of-pocket costs are higher.** Evaluations typically involve a higher total charge than a single therapy session. Patients and families need accurate cost estimates before they commit.
- **Coordination of benefits must be established.** If the client has two insurance plans, which one is primary? Submitting to the wrong payer first delays everything.

**The rule at Twilight Psychology:** Insurance eligibility is verified before the intake appointment and re-verified before testing or any new service type begins. A client who presents with active insurance at intake may have a coverage gap, plan change, or Medicaid redetermination by the time testing or a new service is scheduled.

## What to Verify Before Mental Health Services

A basic eligibility check confirms that the client is covered. For psychological testing — and other services with significant cost or authorization requirements — you need more than that.

### Minimum Information to Collect

- **Member ID and group number** — exactly as on the insurance card
- **Plan effective date and termination date** — is the policy currently active?
- **Coverage type** — commercial, Medicaid (which managed care plan?), Medicare, TRICARE, etc.
- **Mental health/behavioral health benefits** — are they carved out to a separate payer?
- **Deductible status** — has the deductible been met? What is the remaining balance?
- **Copay and coinsurance for the specific service type** — the rate for 96130/96131 evaluation codes may differ from the standard therapy copay; medication management visits use E/M codes with separate cost-sharing
- **Out-of-pocket maximum** — has it been met?
- **Pre-authorization requirements** — is prior auth required for CPT codes 96130, 96131, 96136, 96137, or 90791? Are therapy codes (90837, 90834) or medication management codes (99214, 99215) subject to PA for this plan?
- **Coordination of benefits** — does the client have a second insurance plan?
- **PSYPACT eligibility** — for telehealth testing or therapy under PSYPACT, is the plan a participating managed care plan that covers telehealth?

### Behavioral Health Carve-Outs

Many commercial plans (particularly large employer plans) **carve out behavioral health benefits** to a separate managed care company. For example:

- A UHC medical plan may carve behavioral health to **Optum** (a UHC subsidiary)
- An Anthem medical plan may carve behavioral health to a separate mental health vendor
- A Humana commercial plan may use **Humana Behavioral Health** as a separate entity from the medical plan

If you call the main member services number on the insurance card and ask about mental health benefits, they may transfer you to the carve-out entity. The carve-out plan may have different deductibles, different prior authorization processes, and different provider networks than the medical plan.

Always confirm: **who pays the mental health claim** and **who manages prior authorization for behavioral health**.

## Methods for Verifying Insurance Eligibility

### 1. Online Payer Portals (Recommended)

Most major payers offer real-time eligibility verification through their provider portals at no additional charge.

| Payer | Portal |
|---|---|
| UnitedHealthcare / Optum | UHCprovider.com → Eligibility & Benefits |
| Humana | Humana.com/provider → Eligibility |
| Aetna Better Health (KY Medicaid) | AetnaBetterHealth.com/kentucky → Provider portal |
| Anthem BCBS | Availity or anthem.com/provider |
| Medicare | Availity → Medicare eligibility check; or CGS IVR |
| Kentucky Medicaid FFS | kymmis.com → 270/271 eligibility transaction |
| TRICARE | Tricare.mil or WPS Government Health Administrators portal (Humana military for Tricare East) |

Portal verification provides a real-time snapshot that can be saved as documentation. For psychological testing specifically, documenting that eligibility was verified before the date of service is important if a coverage dispute arises later.

### 2. EHR-Integrated Eligibility (SimplePractice)

SimplePractice supports automated eligibility verification via the 270/271 HIPAA transaction through Availity. Setting up eligibility verification in the EHR allows the system to batch-check upcoming appointments before each day of service. The result populates in the client's billing record.

EHR-integrated checks are efficient for high-volume practices, but always review the returned data. Automated checks sometimes return a generic "active" status without capturing the specific benefit details (deductible status, pre-auth requirements) that matter for testing claims.

### 3. Phone Verification

Calling the member services number on the insurance card is slower but sometimes necessary when:
- Online verification returns incomplete data
- The plan has recently changed and the portal data is not yet updated
- You need to ask specific questions about prior authorization requirements
- The client's COB situation is unclear

When calling, request a **reference number** for the call and document: the agent's name, call date, time, eligibility confirmation, and any specific statements about authorization requirements.

### 4. Automated IVR Systems

Some payers (Noridian for Medicare, some Medicaid plans) offer automated Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems that return eligibility data by entering the NPI and member ID via phone keypad. IVR calls are faster than speaking with an agent but do not provide the same level of detail as a portal lookup.

## Coordination of Benefits (COB)

When a client has two insurance plans, the correct order of billing is determined by **coordination of benefits** rules. Submitting to the wrong payer first will result in denial or reduced payment.

### Primary vs. Secondary Payer — How to Determine Order

The standard COB rules:

- **The member's own insurance is primary** for services they receive. The spouse's plan (where the member is a dependent) is secondary.
- For a **dependent child** with coverage under both parents' plans, the **birthday rule** typically applies: the parent whose birthday falls earlier in the calendar year has the primary plan. Some plans use the gender rule instead — verify with the specific payer.
- For a client with **Medicare and Medicaid**, Medicare is almost always primary and Medicaid is secondary (Medicaid is the payer of last resort).
- For a client with **Medicare and commercial insurance**, the order depends on employment status and employer size (FICA MSP rules). Medicare may be secondary to an employer group health plan.

### COB for Medicaid

Kentucky Medicaid (KY Medicaid FFS and managed care plans) has specific COB requirements. If the client has commercial insurance, Medicaid **requires that the commercial plan be billed first**. Billing Medicaid first when a commercial plan is primary is a billing compliance violation. The commercial plan must be billed, a remittance obtained, and the Medicaid secondary claim submitted with the primary EOB attached.

For psychological testing, this sequencing affects timely filing. The time spent waiting for the primary commercial EOB must not push the secondary Medicaid claim past its timely filing window.

### What Happens When COB Is Not Established Before Services

If COB is not established and the claim is submitted to the wrong payer:
- The claim will be denied with a COB denial reason code
- You will need to re-submit to the correct primary payer
- If the re-submission falls outside timely filing, the claim may be unrecoverable
- The patient may end up with a larger balance than expected if the secondary payer needed the primary's payment first

## Pre-Authorization: What to Do if It Is Required

For payers that require prior authorization for psychological testing (covered in detail in [the next post in this series](/blog/insurance/prior-authorization-psychological-testing)), eligibility verification is also the trigger for the PA process.

When eligibility verification reveals that the plan requires pre-authorization for 96130, 96131, or other testing codes:

1. **Do not schedule the testing session until the PA is obtained.** Services provided without required authorization are non-covered and typically not billable to the patient.
2. Initiate the authorization request with the clinical information gathered at intake (90791).
3. Track the authorization approval and record the authorization number before the testing date.
4. Confirm that the authorization covers the **specific CPT codes, number of units, and dates of service** you plan to bill.

### What You Cannot Charge the Patient for Services Without Authorization

If a pre-authorization was required and not obtained, the insurance plan will typically deny the claim. In most plan contracts, the provider cannot then bill the patient for the denied service — it becomes a **provider write-off**. The exception would be if the patient was clearly informed in advance that authorization had not been obtained and that they would be responsible for the cost. Even then, network contracts may prohibit patient billing for non-authorized services.

This is one of the most financially significant risks in psychological testing billing. A single denied evaluation claim without authorization can represent hundreds of dollars in uncollectible services.

## Documenting Eligibility Verification

Keep a record of every eligibility verification:
- Method used (portal, phone, EHR)
- Date and time of verification
- What was confirmed (active coverage, deductible status, PA requirement, COB)
- Reference number if a phone call was made

This documentation is valuable if a payer later claims coverage was not in effect on the date of service, or if an audit requires proof that the practice acted in good faith based on available eligibility information.
