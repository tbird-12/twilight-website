> **Disclaimer:** Prior authorization requirements change frequently. Always verify current requirements directly with each payer before scheduling psychological testing services. Coverage rules described here reflect general experience and may not reflect current payer policies.


Not every insurance plan requires prior authorization before mental health services. But several plans that are common in Kentucky do — and for those plans, providing services without first obtaining approval can result in claim denial and a write-off that cannot be billed to the patient.

This applies to [psychological evaluations](/services/psychological-evaluations/), but also extends to [therapy](/services/therapy/) and [medication management](/services/medication-management/) in some plans. The stakes are highest for evaluations because of the total cost involved; however, the PA principles described here apply broadly across mental health services.

This is the fifth post in our series on billing for mental health services in Kentucky.

## Why Pre-Authorization Exists for Mental Health Services

Psychological testing is an expensive, time-intensive service. A full neuropsychological or psychoeducational evaluation can involve 10–20 hours of clinician time across intake, testing, interpretation, report writing, and feedback. The total insurance reimbursement for a complete evaluation can be several hundred to several thousand dollars depending on the payer and the codes billed.

Because of the cost and complexity, many payers use prior authorization to:
- Confirm the evaluation is medically necessary before it is performed
- Ensure the requesting clinician is credentialed and in-network
- Limit testing to cases where a clear clinical rationale is documented
- Control the number of testing hours approved per episode of care

PA requirements also exist for some therapy and medication management services — particularly for specialty therapy modalities, initial psychiatric evaluations under certain plans, or when a member has exceeded standard visit limits. The consequences of not obtaining required PA are the same regardless of service type: claim denial and a provider write-off.

## Plans That Require Prior Authorization in Kentucky

The following plans commonly require pre-authorization for **psychological testing services**, particularly for CPT codes **96130** (psychological testing evaluation services, first hour) and **96131** (each additional hour), **96136/96137** (psychological testing administration and scoring). PA requirements for therapy and medication management are noted where applicable. PA is often required for medication at the pharmacy benefit level as well but that is outside the scope of this post.

### Aetna Better Health of Kentucky (Medicaid Managed Care)

**Aetna Better Health** is a Medicaid managed care plan in Kentucky. Prior authorization is required for psychological testing, including 96130 and 96131. The PA request must include clinical documentation of medical necessity. Aetna Better Health may also require PA for certain therapy modalities or extended therapy when clinical criteria are not met through standard outpatient therapy benefit rules.

Aetna Better Health also has specific documentation requirements for prior authorization appeals when a request is initially denied or modified. See our [detailed Aetna Better Health post](/blog/insurance/aetna-better-health-in-network) for a closer look at their PA and appeals process.

### UnitedHealthcare (UHC) — Commercial and Managed Care

**UHC commercial plans and UHC Medicaid managed care** both require prior authorization for psychological testing evaluation codes. This includes UHC Community Plan, which covers Medicaid members in some states.

A specific UHC billing rule for psychological testing: **all testing service dates must be billed on the same claim**. UHC requires that 90791 (intake), 96136/96137 (administration and scoring), 96130/96131 (evaluation services), and the feedback session codes be submitted together, typically at the time of the feedback session. Billing testing codes piecemeal across separate dates on separate claims can result in denials for this payer.

The clinical rationale for this UHC policy is that the evaluation is considered a single episode of care. In practice, this means:
- Hold all claims for a UHC testing case until the feedback session is complete
- Submit all service dates and codes together on one claim or linked claims
- Ensure the authorization covers all planned CPT codes and service dates

### WellCare (Medicaid Managed Care)

**WellCare** (now part of Centene Corporation) provides Medicaid managed care in Kentucky. Prior authorization is typically required for 96130 and 96131. The authorization process involves submitting a clinical request that includes the diagnosis, clinical history, and specific testing requested.

### Ambetter (Commercial Marketplace Plan via Centene)

**Ambetter** marketplace plans also require prior authorization for psychological testing evaluation codes. Ambetter plans are purchased through the ACA marketplace and may cover individuals who do not qualify for Medicaid but purchase subsidized insurance. These plans can be encountered in private practice when a client has marketplace coverage through a Centene-affiliated plan.

### Plans That Typically Do Not Require Prior Authorization

- **Kentucky Medicaid FFS (fee-for-service):** Generally does not require PA for standard psychological testing codes, though medical necessity documentation standards still apply.
- **Medicare:** Medicare does not require prior authorization for most outpatient psychological testing or therapy services. Medical necessity must be documented in the record, but a formal PA is not required.
- **Humana commercial:** Many Humana commercial plans do not require PA for psychological testing or standard outpatient therapy, but benefit limitations and deductible tracking still apply. Verify at the time of eligibility check.
- **TRICARE:** PA requirements vary by TRICARE plan type. TRICARE Prime typically requires a referral; TRICARE Select does not, at the time of this post.

> Plans change their PA requirements, and managed care plans within the same parent company may have different rules. Always verify at the time of eligibility verification, not based on past experience alone.

## What Clinical Documentation Is Needed for a PA Request

For payers that require prior authorization, the request must demonstrate **medical necessity** — the clinical reason why this specific evaluation is needed for this specific patient at this time.

A standard PA request typically includes:

- **Patient identifying information:** name, date of birth, member ID
- **Provider information:** billing NPI, group NPI, rendering provider NPI and taxonomy
- **Requested CPT codes and number of units:** e.g., 96130 × 1 unit + 96131 × 3 units for a 4-hour evaluation
- **Proposed dates of service**
- **Primary diagnosis code (ICD-10):** the presenting diagnosis that justifies testing
- **Clinical summary:** symptoms, duration, functional impact, reason testing was requested
- **Prior treatment:** what has already been tried or ruled out
- **Specific clinical question:** what question the evaluation is intended to answer

Some payers request the 90791 intake note or a clinical letter from the referring provider. For evaluations requested by a school, pediatrician, or psychiatrist, including that referral documentation strengthens the request.

### Tips for Stronger PA Submissions

- Be specific about the **clinical question** the evaluation must answer. "Rule out ADHD" is weaker than "differential diagnosis of attention and executive function impairment affecting academic performance and daily functioning in a 12-year-old with a prior therapy history."
- Document **functional impairment** — insurance companies respond more favorably to evidence that the condition is affecting school, work, or daily life.
- Request **adequate units.** For a comprehensive evaluation, 96130 × 1 and 96131 × 2–4 is common depending on scope. Requesting fewer units than you expect to use creates the need for a mid-evaluation authorization amendment.
- Include the **feedback session codes** if the PA system requires all planned codes upfront (some payers want 90834 or 90846 included in the authorization).

## Tracking Authorization Approvals

When authorization is approved, record and store:

- **Authorization number** — required on the claim form (Box 23 on the CMS-1500)
- **Authorized CPT codes** — which codes were approved? Was 96131 approved for the number of units you requested?
- **Dates of service window** — authorization is typically valid for 90 days; testing must occur within that window
- **Number of units approved** — do not exceed approved units without an amendment

If the authorization approves fewer units than you planned to use, contact the payer before the evaluation begins to request an amendment. Using more units than authorized results in a denial for the excess units.

## What to Do When a PA Is Denied or Delayed

### If Authorization Is Delayed

Most payers have a standard turnaround window for PA decisions: 3–7 business days for non-urgent requests, 24–72 hours for urgent (expedited) requests. If you have not received a determination within the expected window:

- Call the payer's prior authorization line and ask for a status update
- Document the call (date, time, reference number, agent name)
- Confirm whether the request was received and what is pending

Do not schedule testing until authorization is confirmed. A verbal indication that authorization is "in process" is not the same as an approved authorization.

### If Authorization Is Denied

A PA denial for psychological testing is typically one of three types:

1. **Not medically necessary:** The payer determined that the clinical documentation did not support the evaluation. This is the most common and most appealable denial.
2. **Administrative denial:** Missing information in the request, incorrect codes, or expired authorization. Correct and resubmit.
3. **Benefit not covered:** The member's plan does not include psychological testing as a covered benefit. This is the hardest to appeal and often results in a self-pay arrangement or a decision not to proceed.

For **not medically necessary** denials, the appeals process typically allows submission of additional clinical documentation. This is covered in detail in [our post on claim denials and appeals](/blog/billing/claim-denials-appeals-psychological-testing).

For **benefit not covered** denials, confirm the denial in writing and clarify whether the denial is a true plan exclusion or an incorrect application of the plan's rules. Mental health parity law (MHPAEA) requires that mental health and substance use disorder benefits not be subject to more restrictive limitations than comparable medical/surgical benefits. If a plan covers comparable medical diagnostic procedures but excludes psychological testing on grounds that appear more restrictive, a parity-based appeal may apply.

## UHC Same-Day Billing Reminder

As mentioned above, UHC's requirement to submit all psychological testing claims together has a practical impact on cash flow and billing workflow. For UHC patients:

- The claim should be held until the **feedback session is complete**
- All CPT codes (90791, 96136/96137, 96130/96131, and 90834 or 90846) are submitted as a single claim covering all service dates
- Copay and coinsurance amounts must be collected accurately for each date of service at the time those appointments occur, even though the claim isn't submitted until the end

This makes patient cost estimates especially important for UHC evaluations. Collecting the intake copay, the testing session copay, and the feedback session copay on the correct dates requires front-of-house awareness that each appointment has its own financial responsibility, even if the billing is held for a combined claim submission.
