In November 2026, Jonica, Ethan, and Heather attended the Kentucky Psychological Association meeting between Nov 5-7. The American Psychological Association shared healthcare financing, coding, and policy updates that affect behavioral health practices nationwide. This post summarizes the most relevant points for clients, referral partners, and operations in Kentucky. The information is based on what was presented at the meeting and summarized by our team.

## Medicare and coding changes to watch

The APA update highlighted proposed 2027 Medicare reimbursement increases for several psychotherapy and health behavior codes, including 90837, 90832, 96158, and 90847. It also reinforced active advocacy around psychological and neuropsychological testing code valuation, where reimbursement often remains below the complexity of the work.

The presentation also reviewed expanded use of services involving auxiliary or clinical staff under Medicare incident-to rules, plus newer code families for integrated care, interprofessional consultation, and digital mental health treatment management. For practices like ours, these updates matter because they influence staffing models, payer contracting strategy, and long-term service access.

## Medicaid and commercial network advocacy

Another major theme was parity and network adequacy. APA cited ongoing disparities in in-network behavioral health access and discussed state-level advocacy efforts tied to Medicaid reimbursement floors, trainee billing pathways, and commercial payer accountability.

For Kentucky practices, this policy direction is important because reimbursement stability affects clinician retention, wait times, and whether specialized services can remain sustainable in-network.

## What this means for Twilight Psychology

Our focus remains the same: maintaining access to high-quality neuro-affirming care while adapting responsibly to reimbursement and policy changes. As federal and state guidance evolves, we continue reviewing billing workflows, code utilization, and payer participation to keep care pathways clear for clients and referral sources.
